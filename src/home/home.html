Three jobs from a real distribution network, turned into games. Each one plays in your browser, on a phone or a computer.

Real-time control room

Losing Control

You're the control engineer for Blackout Canyon's network for one shift. Transformers trip, feeders fault, storms roll in and demand peaks at teatime. Switch load between substations, send out field crews, bring in generators and get every customer back on supply. At the end of the shift, the regulator marks your work.

  • Shifts from a quiet Monday to storms and busbar faults
  • A daily challenge, a career and a scoreboard
  • Generation shifts: balance solar, wind, batteries and flexibility
Play Losing Control →

Fault-finding puzzle

Fault Lines

An 11 kV feeder has tripped and locked out, and everyone on it is off. Read the clues (protection flags, fault passage indicators and customer calls), then open and close switches to find the faulted section in as few steps as you can. Isolate it and get everyone else back on.

  • A new puzzle every day, the same for everyone. Share your result grid
  • A 30-puzzle career, from trainee to senior control engineer
  • Unlimited practice at easy, medium and hard
Play Fault Lines →

Ten-year planning game

Gatekeeper

Ten years as Blackout Canyon's connections and planning engineer. Solar farms, batteries, EV hubs and data centres all want a connection. Offer firm or flexible terms, reinforce the network or say not yet, and keep the lights on as the queue grows.

  • A report card each year on connections, security, curtailment and cost
  • Queue reform, cold winters, storms and a big customer who pulls out
  • A new canyon every week, with its own scoreboard

Welcome to Blackout Canyon

All three games share one made-up network: the Steve Towers grid supply point, the Blackout Canyon 33 kV substation, and the primaries at Hertz House, Shocking Place, Ohm No and Joule Street (and, on some shifts, Kilowatt Close), with links to Ampy Place and Watts This. The places are invented. The switching, the faults and the trade-offs are based on how real distribution networks are run.
